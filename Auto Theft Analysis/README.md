# Ottawa Auto Theft Analysis Report
### 2018 – 2023

**Prepared by:** Olamilekan Elegbede
**Date:** May 4, 2026
**Data Source:** Ottawa Police Service

---

## Executive Summary

Car theft in Ottawa has reached crisis levels, rising **124.4%** over the five-year period from 2018 to 2023. On average, **four vehicles are stolen every day** across the city. In 2023 alone, the Ottawa Police Service recorded **1,994 stolen vehicles**, representing an estimated **$26.6 million in loss value**, with only **57%** of those vehicles recovered.

This report analyzes auto theft trends across four dimensions — temporal patterns, geographical distribution, vehicle attributes, and recovery rates — and concludes with actionable recommendations for key stakeholders.

Auto theft data in this report covers all vehicle types, including cars, SUVs, trucks, motorcycles, vans, trailers, ATVs, snowmobiles, and motor homes.

**Top priorities for reducing auto theft in Ottawa:**
1. Preventing thefts through effective anti-theft measures by vehicle manufacturers.
2. Strengthening information sharing and data-driven partnerships among law enforcement and communities.

## 1. Temporal Analysis

Auto theft in Ottawa has followed a largely upward trajectory since 2018, reaching a record high in 2022 before a marginal decline in 2023.

### Key Findings

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total thefts, 2018–2023 | 7,826 |
| Record high year | 2022 (2,033 thefts) |
| YoY change, 2022–2023 | -2.1% |
| Overall increase, 2018–2023 | +123.8% |
| Peak months | October & November (~5 thefts/day) |
| Weekend share of thefts | 26.3% |
| Evening/nighttime thefts (8 PM – 3 AM) | >58% |

### Analysis

Following the record high of **2,033 thefts in 2022**, Ottawa saw a slight year-over-year decrease of **2.1%** in 2023. Despite this, the overall trend since 2018 represents a **123.8% increase**, indicating a persistent and deepening problem.

Theft rates peak in **October and November**, reaching approximately five thefts per day. There is no strong seasonal pattern, though a dip is observed at the start of each year.

Weekdays account for the majority (**73.7%**) of thefts, suggesting that vehicles are more vulnerable during regular work and commute hours when owners may be less vigilant. However, the highest-risk time window is **evenings and nighttime (8 PM – 3 AM)**, which accounts for over **58%** of all thefts — with **40%** occurring specifically during evening hours.

## 2. Geographical Analysis

Theft activity is concentrated in residential areas in Ottawa's East and West divisions, with distinct patterns linked to proximity to export infrastructure.

### Regional Theft Growth (2018–2023)

| Division | Theft Increase |
|---|---|
| West Ottawa | +205.7% |
| East Ottawa | +139.2% |
| Central Ottawa | +30.9% |

### Key Findings

- **East Ottawa** reported the highest volume of thefts, accounting for over **41%** of all incidents. Its proximity to the **Port of Montreal** — a major hub for overseas vehicle exports — is a key contributing factor.
- **Barrhaven, Orleans, and Centretown** were the highest-risk neighbourhoods, with a combined estimated loss of approximately **$17.7 million**.
- Theft density is highest in **densely populated residential neighbourhoods** in the East and West, while Central Ottawa — with its higher proportion of commercial activity — saw comparatively lower growth.

## 3. Vehicle Attributes

Organized crime groups are systematically targeting newer, higher-value vehicles for overseas resale, driving both theft volumes and financial losses.

### Most Targeted Brands

| Make | Share of Thefts | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Toyota | 21.8% | Most targeted; 2018/19/21/22 Highlander SUV most stolen model |
| Honda | Top 5 | High combined loss value |
| Ford | Top 5 | High combined loss value |
| Jeep | Top 5 | High combined loss value |
| Lexus | Top 5 | High combined loss value |

Honda, Ford, Jeep, and Lexus collectively represent a loss value of **$36.6 million**.

### Vehicle Age

- **52.9%** of stolen vehicles were manufactured in **2017 or newer**, representing **$76.7 million** in total loss value.
- Newer vehicles are preferentially targeted by organized crime rings seeking maximum resale profit in overseas markets.
- The recovery rate for vehicles manufactured 2017 or newer is **50.9%**, suggesting a high proportion are being exported before recovery is possible.

### Vehicle Type

- **Automobiles** (sedans, coupes, hatchbacks) were the most frequently stolen vehicle type, accounting for **58.7%** of all thefts.

## 4. Recovery Rates

Despite a modest year-over-year improvement in 2022–2023, the long-term trend in vehicle recovery rates is declining, pointing to increasingly sophisticated criminal operations.

### Recovery Rate Trend

| Year | Recovery Rate |
|---|---|
| 2018 | 63.9% |
| 2023 | 55.7% |
| Overall (2018–2023) | 57.2% |
| YoY change (2022–2023) | +2.9% |

### Recovery Rate by Make (min. 200 reported thefts)

| Make | Recovery Rate | Avg. Stolen Value | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyundai | 83.1% | ~$3,131 | Likely stolen for use in other crimes, not resale |
| Lexus | 40.2% | ~$13,170 | Likely exported overseas by organized groups |

- Of **483 stolen Lexus vehicles**, only **179** were recovered — a recovery rate of just **40.2%**.
- **Vehicles manufactured 2017 or newer** had a significantly lower recovery rate (**51.4%**) compared to older vehicles (**71.9%**), for models with at least 100 reported thefts.
- Toyota and Lexus, though among the most stolen brands, have disproportionately low recovery rates — underscoring the role of organized export operations and the need for enhanced tracking and anti-theft systems.

## 5. Solutions and Recommendations

### 5.1 Enhanced Security in High-Risk Areas
Increase police and security presence in high-risk neighbourhoods — particularly in **East Ottawa** — during the peak theft window of **8 PM to 3 AM**. Targeted patrols in Barrhaven, Orleans, and Centretown should be prioritized.

### 5.2 Improved Vehicle Anti-Theft and Tracking Features
Engage automakers — particularly **Toyota, Honda, Ford, Jeep, and Lexus** — to:
- Address known security vulnerabilities exploited by criminal groups (e.g., relay attack weaknesses in keyless entry systems).
- Improve embedded vehicle tracking capabilities to support law enforcement recovery efforts.
- Implement stronger immobilization technology in high-demand models.

### 5.3 Community Awareness and Information Sharing
- Launch targeted awareness campaigns in high-risk neighbourhoods, focusing on theft prevention measures such as steering wheel locks, driveway cameras, and signal-blocking key pouches.
- Strengthen data-sharing partnerships between the Ottawa Police Service, Transport Canada, the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), and Port of Montreal authorities to intercept export-bound stolen vehicles.
